Causality and resources in Molten

Current semantics: Types are computation — open the iterative program laboratory. The formal plan supersedes this report’s earlier separation of proof search and event commitment. Its causal and resource counterexamples remain relevant.

A computation may retain its entire past without retaining the right to spend that past again. Molten needs to represent causal history, mutually exclusive derivations, and currently usable resources separately. One lineage equivalence class cannot encode all three. A compatible history can include a divergence and its later decoherence; it cannot include two alternative events that both spend the same occurrence.

Coherences evolve independently in parallel. A comma separates their expressions. A compatible configuration is a mathematical record spanning those coherences; it is not a replacement definition of “coherence.” An explicit rule may decohere compatible versions. The proposal preserves their separate identities while making their permitted interactions precise.

The strongest starting point is an occurrence-based semantics with explicit resource conflict. It captures the intended distinction between forward evolution and combining incompatible versions of one computation. It also exposes a second issue: broadcasting unmatched concepts can create exponential duplication even when every decoherence is causally valid. That issue needs a decision about what a concept occurrence means.

This report distinguishes established results from Molten-specific proposals. The experiments are finite executable design models, not a Molten runtime or a simulation of physical spacetime. All controls work offline. Arrow notation describes rules; it does not introduce a new source-language token.

Core vocabulary

Rules describe change and establish concepts for matching. Events record particular committed changes.

Use input, output, and apply for rules. Divergence creates several coherences; decoherence combines compatible coherences. These describe interaction shapes, not additional executable constructs. Ordinary multiset union remains merge.

Runtime details use version for a particular coherence state, history for its causal record, origin for a resource's introduction, and configuration for a compatible selection across coherences. A proof justifies a match through its concrete witnesses. Origin tracking and abstract matching remain proposals.

The core idea

Keep independent contexts, preserve their histories, and combine only compatible uses of those histories. A shared past is allowed. Spending the same consumable occurrence incompatibly is not allowed in one resulting history. Retaining evidence of an old state does not restore permission to consume it.

Under the leading proposal, carrying one inherited X through two coherences does not manufacture a second origin when they combine. Independently introduced Xs still count separately. If one coherence consumes X while another carries it, their reconciliation must not resurrect it. Exact read and consumption rules remain open; no hidden cross-coherence mutation is implied.

Abstraction belongs inside the language. A program can establish that True satisfies Boolean and then match Boolean through that evidence. The proposed matcher binds the original True occurrence; discovering another description does not create another consumable particle. Two proofs about one occurrence cannot satisfy two independent inputs. We must specify how establishing a concept differs from transforming state before choosing syntax. This is part of the first runtime slice.

Forward evolution permits fresh events with repeated labels and can continue indefinitely. Preventing accidental duplication does not forbid explicit production. The current kernel still implements the old broadcasting algebra; these proposed laws are not yet runtime behavior. See the concise core design.

Coherence and decoherence are language-specific names. Physical decoherence concerns environmental suppression of interference, not combining computational contexts. Molten's interaction rules must stand on their computational definitions. [15]

Two outputs from X → [Y, Z] share a producer but occupy distinct coherences. By contrast, the competing rules X → [Y, Q] and X → [Z, R] each consume the same version of X. Y and Z then occupy different coherence contexts and neither is an ancestor of the other, yet their histories are incompatible under the proposed consuming semantics. Distinct coherence numbers plus an ancestry check would still miss this conflict.

Occurrence nets formalize this using conditions, events, causal paths, and conflict inherited from competing consumptions. Their concurrency relation allows conditions that can hold together at a reachable marking. This supplies a precise vocabulary for the distinction Molten's lineage prose is reaching for. [1]

Event structures also separate causality from conflict: a configuration records a causally closed, conflict-free history. Shared ancestry is therefore not itself a reason to reject a decoherence. In the finite consuming examples above, compatible frontiers can be checked directly by enumerating configurations; a production engine would avoid exhaustive enumeration. [2]

Physical interpretation

The computational requirement is compatible use of history. Physical intuition motivates Molten’s causal and resource constraints; explicit computational laws let us test how far each correspondence holds. Causality, resource exclusivity, and conservation are separate constraints.

Causality is a partial order

Lamport's happened-before relation models local order and actual communication without assuming a universal clock. His paper explicitly distinguishes actual messages in a computation from the possible signaling relation used in relativity. This is a suitable analogy for Molten: establish dependencies from events, and treat a scheduler's total order as an implementation choice rather than a new semantic law. [3]

Causal set theory proposes a locally finite partial order as a fundamental spacetime structure. It is an approach to quantum gravity, not an experimentally established instruction set for a programming language. Its relevant lesson here is modest: directed causality and finite causal neighborhoods can be clean primitives. Its local finiteness assumption does not imply that an entire history has finite length. [4]

Molten does not currently model distance, light cones, spacetime geometry, probability amplitudes, or a physical conservation quantity. Calling two compatible particles “concurrent” should consequently mean that the resource model permits them together, not that they are statistically independent or physically spacelike separated. A common cause can correlate two outputs while still allowing them to participate in one later event.

Coherences and alternative histories

Branching space-time theories distinguish possible events, histories, and local indeterminism. Belnap, Müller, and Placek show that even the choice between branching points and branching sets needs explicit axioms. Their results do not select a unique interaction law for Molten. A coherence names a parallel context in the language; a separate compatibility relation governs which histories may interact. [13]

Griffiths' consistent-histories formulation restricts combining incompatible quantum frameworks. It offers a useful philosophical parallel to refusing a conclusion assembled from incompatible descriptions, but its compatibility conditions concern Hilbert-space histories and probabilities. They are not the same as Molten's proposed resource-conflict predicate. This report adopts neither that interpretation nor an Everettian ontology as a physical requirement. [14]

Quantum no-cloning is a specific theorem

Wootters and Zurek showed that quantum linearity prevents universal replication of arbitrary quantum states. The result concerns quantum states and permitted physical operations; its assumptions are absent from Molten's present multiset algebra. [5] IBM's formal presentation requires one operation that clones every input state, and derives a contradiction from linearity. A classical label such as Ready is not an arbitrary unknown quantum state. [6]

The useful design question is therefore whether duplicating Ready duplicates information, an authorization to act, or a physical resource modeled by the program. Those interpretations have different laws. Preventing one authorization from being spent twice is a meaningful language guarantee without claiming to implement quantum mechanics.

Forward evolution need not erase history

Bennett's reversible computation construction demonstrates that computation can preserve enough information to reverse its steps, and can later remove intermediate history through uncomputation. Retaining a derivation graph is thus compatible with a forward operational interface; retaining history does not mean old resources remain spendable. Conversely, an append-only log alone does not prove physical reversibility. [7]

For Molten, “forward” can mean that each event has fresh identity and depends only on an already established finite history. A sequence A₀ → B₀ → A₁ → B₁ moves forward in occurrence identity even though its labels repeat. Whether to stop such a sequence is a separate question about budgets, observable equivalence, or the admitted language fragment.

The divergence–decoherence counterexample

The old inference specification says to concatenate every unmatched remainder and broadcast it to every output. Consider two rules, [A] → [B, C] and [B, C] → A, starting with A.X. The divergence carries X into both branches. The decoherence finds two unmatched copies and returns A.X.X. Every round repeats this doubling.

A.X
  → B.X, C.X
  → A.X.X
  → B.X.X, C.X.X
  → A.X.X.X.X

This is a direct derivation from Molten's documented algebra, rather than a claim borrowed from physics. No stale ancestor participates in these decoherences. A causal eligibility check will correctly permit the sibling interaction, so it cannot repair this multiplication. The Rust kernel includes a characterization test demonstrating one round.

A small impossibility result for the current combination

Assume four requirements: unmatched concept multiplicity is preserved; every divergence copies the full remainder to every output; sibling outputs may decohere; decohering adds their remainders. Under these assumptions, the two-rule example necessarily doubles X. No change to a lineage data structure can make all four assumptions true and also preserve the count of X. At least one requirement must change if implicit duplication is prohibited.

Deduplicating by label is not an acceptable repair. Two independently supplied occurrences X₀ and X₁ must still count as two, while two references to X₀ must not become two spendable resources. A content hash can share storage; it cannot decide resource identity.

Three coherent directions

Copying values preserves the existing rewrite algebra. It is reasonable if labels describe freely copyable classical data and exponential growth is intentional. The language must then promise prevention of stale-state reuse, not prevention of all duplication. Resource-sensitive applications would need an additional concept of ownership.

Sharing carried identities keeps the apparent broadcasting syntax but carries references to original concept occurrences. Two independent X inputs remain distinct; two branch references to the same X remain one resource. This may suit a uniform conceptual language, but it is a research proposal: consuming that X in one branch must affect the eligibility of other references. Merely unioning identifiers at decoherence is insufficient.

The philosophical distinction is between a shared past and independent creation. On this interpretation, B.X and C.X each contain a coherence-relative view of one inherited X. Both coherences retain their own local state; their later reconciliation does not justify inventing an additional origin. Explicit production of a new X introduces a fresh occurrence, and two independently introduced Xs remain two. Thus the leading recommendation for this specific round trip is A.X, while preserving separate coherences throughout the divergence.

This does not impose one global mutable ownership flag. Separate coherences may explore different consumptions of the inherited occurrence. The proposed restriction applies when their histories interact: incompatible uses must not be combined as though they came from independent origins. A valid implementation must distinguish exploring an alternative from authorizing a combined history.

Preserving an outer frame treats unmatched context as one unchanged part of the surrounding configuration. It is included as a comparison, not a recommendation to change the coherence contract: taking X out of both coherences would change their local context. Separation logic's frame rule supplies a related discipline for local reasoning, preserving a disjoint surrounding assertion subject to side conditions. It does not automatically define Molten's scope semantics. [8]

Shared reads also require care. Contextual nets explicitly distinguish checking a resource from consuming it; readers and consumers have interaction constraints beyond a plain consuming net. That is relevant if Molten allows a branch to inspect a carried concept without owning its consumption. The analogy supports investigating a richer access model, not assuming ordinary occurrence-net conflict will cover it unchanged. [9]

A minimal proposed semantics

Keep coherences, version their state, and record compatibility separately. This narrows the first runtime specification to something precise enough to test. The following is a Molten-specific proposal for an ordinary consuming fragment. It does not yet cover read arcs, persistent proofs, higher-order scope, or quantum behavior.

Separate a particle's canonical content from a particular occurrence carrying that content. Separate a reusable rule from an event that applies it once. An event consumes a finite collection of distinct occurrence identities and produces fresh occurrence identities. Two equal output particles are still two outputs when the rule explicitly produces both.

Content       an immutable multiset of concept values
Coherence     one independently evolving parallel context
Version       one occurrence of a coherence's evolving state
Rule          a reusable transformation description
Event         one application with a concrete input binding
Configuration compatible history and frontier across coherence contexts

For a configuration C, its frontier is the set of initial and produced occurrences whose consuming event is absent from C. A candidate event is enabled when every required occurrence is in that frontier and the multiset patterns match. Committing it removes those occurrences from that frontier and adds the newly produced ones. Old records remain available for explanations.

When exploring alternatives, the same occurrence can have several potential consumers in the overall possibility graph. Those consumers must never both belong to one configuration. A global consumed boolean would incorrectly erase alternative histories; consumption is relative to a chosen configuration. An execution mode that commits to one history can use a simpler live set, but that mode is a policy over the richer semantics.

Eligibility and a useful proof obligation

For the finite ordinary consuming fragment, require candidate versions to belong to distinct coherences, be causally incomparable, and be free of inherited consumption conflict. The graph laboratory calculates ancestry and conflict from the event graph and independently enumerates reachable frontiers as a witness. Matching labels is still necessary; eligibility alone does not imply that a rule matches.

The principal safety claim should be: within a compatible configuration, each consumable occurrence is consumed at most once. This follows inductively for the proposed transition rule. Initially no occurrence has been spent. Each admitted event requires its inputs to be live and removes them, so a later event in that configuration cannot consume those same identities. Fresh production does not revive an old identity.

This claim is deliberately narrower than “no duplication.” A rule A → [A, A] explicitly produces two new resources and can grow forever. If that must be forbidden too, impose a conserved weight, a typing constraint, or a decreasing measure on admitted rules. For example, a positive weight satisfying total output weight ≤ total input weight for every event bounds live resource weight from finite input; it still does not imply termination. The counterexample A → B → A can preserve weight while running forever.

Coherences and observables

A coherence retains its meaning as a parallel context. A proposed unary transition produces a fresh version within that context; a divergence produces distinct child coherences; a decoherence produces a successor context while preserving the participating histories. A configuration records which coherence versions can coexist. One version may participate in several alternative configurations, so a coherence identifier is useful but does not replace compatibility checking.

A more economical representation

Represent every version with a reference to immutable content, its coherence identity, and its producing event. A divergence can create several small coherence records referring to the same inherited content. Each branch records its changes independently. This gives semantic separation without eagerly copying all labels or the entire ancestral graph. Storage sharing never makes two coherences equal and never decides whether their X values count once or twice at a decoherence.

For ordinary consuming transitions, a useful prototype is a persistent map from consumed version identity to consuming event identity. Histories agree when common keys name the same event; a different consumer for one version exposes a conflict. Both child coherences of one divergence inherit the same commitment and remain compatible. Competing derivations carry different commitments. A separate frontier check rejects a past version that a selected history has already spent. This is a proposed data representation to validate against the reference model, not a proven complexity improvement.

If inherited concepts keep original occurrence identities, structural sharing can additionally preserve that provenance: merge equal origins once while retaining distinct original occurrences with equal labels. That would change the old additive remainder behavior and must be selected deliberately. If the language instead intends independent copies in each coherence, retain separate logical identities even when their memory representation is shared.

A difficult boundary remains: one coherence consumes inherited X₀, while another coherence merely carries its old view of X₀. A decoherence must not resurrect X₀ by copying that view into a live result. The conservative proposal rejects such a decoherence when it requires the spent origin as a live resource. A more permissive design could distinguish historical descriptions from live resources, but that introduces another semantic distinction. This boundary must be tested before claiming the origin-sharing proposal is complete.

There are at least three different equivalences worth naming precisely. Equal contents can share immutable storage. Equivalent live configurations might be merged for a particular reachability query if that abstraction preserves the query. Equivalent histories require a chosen observation model, including whether provenance matters. Merging by content inside the execution store would silently choose all three at once.

Zero-input rules need an explicit activation occurrence, invocation, or other event policy. Otherwise no consumed identity distinguishes one activation from the next. A bounded evaluator should report exhaustion separately from quiescence, and preserve its agenda so resumption neither skips candidates nor recommits an existing event.

Proof, polymorphism, and resources

Open the iterative program laboratory → Nineteen concrete programs show ordinary events enabling further rules, with retained witnesses, repeated operands, long chains, and cycles.

Molten's existing examples use derivation both to transform state and to justify an abstract match. Those operations can share a language surface while retaining different semantic roles. Learning that an existing True supports a Boolean pattern should not automatically mint another independent Boolean resource.

Linear logic is useful here because it controls structural duplication and disposal of assumptions rather than treating every proposition as freely reusable. Pfenning's presentation makes available resources explicit in judgments. [10] A connection between Petri-net evolution and a fragment of linear logic has also been developed formally; resource transitions and logical descriptions need not be competing foundations. [11]

Simmons and Pfenning distinguish linear from persistent propositions in a forward-chaining language. Their separation restriction requires a linear premise when a rule produces linear conclusions, helping avoid uncontrolled flooding from persistent knowledge alone. Their work also connects operational rules to a cost semantics. This is a particularly relevant precedent for combining Molten's inference ambitions with resource-sensitive execution. [12]

One witness, several descriptions

Suppose occurrence t₀ is directly True, and a reusable derivation proves that True satisfies Boolean. Matching Boolean should retain a reference to t₀ and its proof. Two different proofs about t₀ do not provide two distinct inputs for a rule requiring [Boolean, Boolean]. A second independently available witness t₁ would.

This suggests representing an abstract match as a witness mapping plus evidence, not as a replacement particle produced solely because a proof was found. Whether applying a rule then consumes the concrete witness, preserves it, or changes only an associated operation must be specified. The existing And.True.True example is not enough to determine that rule for all scopes and decoherence.

A proof graph can grow monotonically while resource configurations evolve through consumption. That separation need not force new keywords immediately. It does require that lowering or typing distinguish a reusable relationship from a consumable occurrence. If one uniform concept model is preferred, occurrence identity and borrowing must carry that distinction explicitly.

Proof identity and event identity

Including every proof object in an event key could make two proofs of the same admissible transition produce two events. Excluding proof identity blindly could collapse observably different derivations. The proposed default is to key execution by the rule and concrete witness binding, retaining alternative justifications as evidence, unless the language deliberately makes proof choice observable.

This is another reason to postpone a global content-based fixed point. A finite fact closure, a finite set of reachable frontiers, and a finite set of event histories are different claims. The same program can have a tiny repeating frontier and an unbounded history. Decide which result an API promises before choosing its deduplication key.

Implementation implications

The selected first milestone is the frontend and runtime design. The repository now contains a generated structural parser, byte-accurate diagnostics, and a small provisional multiset kernel. It does not lower source into executable rules or implement graph evaluation. The kernel characterizes the previous broadcast semantics so the design disagreement remains reproducible.

Keep the frontend independent

The parser preserves spelling, whitespace, punctuation, and nested source ranges in a flat vector. This avoids per-label string allocation and recursive tree destruction. Parsing rejects malformed delimiters and excessive nesting before entering the recursive grammar. Structural validity is not semantic validity: empty coherences and repeated dots remain visible for a later lowering pass to interpret or reject.

The build follows Registry's pinned Rust and hermetic Bazel setup. Crates handle parsing, diagnostics, errors, and command arguments. The language core owns only its language-specific data and operations. Performance claims should remain about established algorithmic behavior until an optimized benchmark suite measures the actual workload.

A small next slice

  1. Write a short formal contract for occurrence identity, consuming transitions, and jointly produced outputs. Resolve the remainder example before lowering general divergences.
  2. Define a source subset with literal concepts, a program-defined abstraction, and a rule that matches it through concrete witnesses. Specify abstraction versus state change; give unsupported scope forms precise semantic diagnostics.
  3. Implement append-only typed occurrence and event storage with a separate configuration frontier. Add a maintained interner; use a generational arena only if deletion actually requires it.
  4. Implement one bounded transition against a supplied configuration. Validate all inputs and budget costs before committing any mutation.
  5. Compare an indexed matcher against a slow exhaustive reference on small graphs. Preserve injective bindings and duplicate pattern multiplicity.
  6. Include proof witnesses, chained abstractions, cyclic proof search, and prevention of duplicate consumption in this first slice. Add broader exploration of alternatives after its contract is stable. Keep single-history execution and possibility-graph exploration explicit.

Index candidate occurrences by required concepts and counts, then schedule only affected rules after a change. High-rank matching can still produce combinatorially many bindings; laziness and candidate budgets matter more than a faster container. Measure candidate visits, allocations, peak memory, and committed events separately so optimization does not hide a semantic change.

For a history-preserving engine, start with dense append-only storage and distinct identifier types. Avoid repeating generic serialization bounds through the core. Keep graph traversal utilities replaceable, but do not mistake a general graph crate for a definition of resource conflict. Provenance and event identity are Molten's semantics, not incidental bookkeeping.

What the experiments establish

The laboratory tests finite hand-authored occurrence graphs. Its causal predicate is compared with exhaustive reachable-frontier enumeration for every subset of revealed particles in those examples. That is a meaningful consistency check for these fixtures, not a proof of completeness for arbitrary Molten programs. The remainder experiment computes the exact closed form of the two-rule example; it does not execute the Rust runtime.

Before expanding scope, add tests for abstract witnesses sharing the same resource, conflicting readers and consumers if sharing is selected, symmetric pattern permutations, zero-input activation, resumable budgets, and live-state equivalence under provenance-sensitive queries. The main technical risk is committing to an identity model that later cannot express these cases without exception rules.

Research decisions

The first decision is the status of the carried X. Should the divergence–decoherence example return A.X or A.X.X? If it returns one X, does that happen because both branches share one occurrence, or because X stays outside their local transformation? Those choices agree on this count and disagree on what either branch may independently consume.

My recommendation is to preserve coherences, use immutable coherence versions with explicit conflict, and reject accidental minting from proof discovery. Structural sharing is compatible with either interpretation of inherited X. Sharing X's logical occurrence identity is the most promising anti-duplication proposal that keeps the inherited context available in each coherence, but its read, consume, and merge laws remain a joint design decision. The outer-frame alternative is retained only to make its semantic tradeoff visible.
